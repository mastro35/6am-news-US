### Georgia School Shooter Colt Gray Pleads Guilty to Murder

Colt Gray, the teenager responsible for the devastating September 2024 mass shooting at Apalachee High School in Winder, Georgia, has pleaded guilty to all 55 charges against him, including four counts of murder. Gray was 14 years old when he shot and killed two classmates and two teachers, and wounded nine others. Now 16, he faces a mandatory sentence of life in prison. During the emotional court hearing, victims' families delivered heart-wrenching statements, describing the "life sentence of grief and trauma" Gray inflicted upon their community. The case has drawn nationwide attention, particularly following the landmark criminal conviction of Gray's father for involuntary manslaughter after he purchased the firearm used in the assault for his son.

### Federal Judge Strikes Down FDA Restrictions on Abortion Pill Mifepristone

U.S. District Judge Robert S. Ballou in Virginia has ruled that the Food and Drug Administration's long-standing restrictions on prescribing the abortion pill mifepristone are unlawful. In a scathing decision, the judge characterized the federal barriers to the medication as "arbitrary and capricious," pointing to overwhelming scientific consensus that mifepristone is safe and highly effective. The ruling orders the Trump administration's FDA to formally re-examine and consider overturning the safety protocols that heavily restrict how the drug can be distributed. Reproductive rights advocates praised the decision as a major victory against mounting state-level abortion bans, while conservative legal groups criticized the court's interference with federal regulatory authority.

### ICC Ousts Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan Following Sexual Misconduct Investigation

The governing body of the International Criminal Court voted to remove British barrister Karim Khan from his role as chief prosecutor. The historic decision follows a lengthy internal crisis over allegations of sexual misconduct involving a junior female staff member. Khan has repeatedly denied the accusations, claiming the investigation was politically motivated to undermine his authority. His removal ends a highly controversial tenure that saw the prosecutor seek high-profile arrest warrants for world leaders, including Vladimir Putin and Benjamin Netanyahu. The ICC's member states finalized the vote to ensure the integrity of the court as it faces unprecedented political pressure and U.S. sanctions against several of its officials.

Hollywood giants Paramount Global and Warner Bros. Discovery have agreed to pause their planned $110 billion merger while a major anti-monopoly lawsuit plays out in federal court. Under the terms of the legal agreement, the two entertainment studios will remain completely separate, competing operations until a judge issues a final verdict, or until June 1, 2027. The delay could trigger hundreds of millions of dollars in fees for Paramount, which has already agreed to pay up to $1.7 billion in penalties if the transaction fails to close.
