## Front Page

### FAA outage grounds flights nationwide, snarling travel into the weekend
A telecommunications failure in aging air-traffic control equipment prompted the FAA to halt departures nationwide for more than four hours Friday. The agency lifted the ground stop, but airlines warned that cancellations and delays would continue as schedules recovered. Officials said early findings pointed to overloaded systems, not a cyberattack; the disruption affected more than 14,500 flights, according to the *Wall Street Journal*.

### Nvidia scandal raises questions about China, Washington and the company’s future
Nvidia founder Jensen Huang’s abrupt departure has sent shock waves through Silicon Valley and Washington. ### Brooklyn building collapse prompts scrutiny of developer oversight

City officials have commissioned an independent engineering report as families press for answers.

### All rescued after tanker strikes New Orleans riverboat
A tanker collided with a docked riverboat on the Mississippi River, leaving the passenger vessel partially submerged. Authorities accounted for everyone aboard, while 32 people were treated at hospitals for noncritical injuries. Officials called the rescue a “miracle on the Mississippi.”

## Politics

### Alaska elects its first Democratic governor since 1998
Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins won Alaska’s governor’s race, ending nearly three decades without a Democratic governor. The coalition-builder has also emphasized the state’s severe fiscal challenges. The state’s Senate contest between Republican Dan Sullivan and Democrat Mary Peltola remained uncalled, with ranked-choice tabulation still underway.
[The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/14/us/elections/alaska-governor-winner-kreiss-tomkins-jkt.html) - [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/14/us/alaska-senate-race-ranked-choice-tabulation.html)

### Supreme Court to consider Trump’s asylum restrictions
The Supreme Court agreed to hear a challenge to the Trump administration’s policy restricting asylum applications at the U.S.-Mexico border. The case will test the limits of presidential authority against the legal right to seek asylum, a central issue in the administration’s immigration agenda.
